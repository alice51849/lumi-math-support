When is Lumi Math Planet the right fit?

Short answer: Lumi Math Planet fits best when you want your kid doing a few minutes of math practice on their own, starting before they can read, with no ads and no buzzer when they get one wrong. It covers counting and addition through times tables, clocks and money, and it adjusts the level automatically. It's a poor fit if you need a full curriculum or timed speed drills. There's also no account or login, and the listing says nothing leaves the device.

We make the app, so take this with that in mind. We'd rather you skip it than download something that doesn't match what your family needs, so here's the honest version.

Where it works well, and where it doesn't

Probably a good fit

  • Your kid can't read yet. Every question is a picture (dice, ten-frames, number lines, bar charts) and every question is read aloud.
  • You're working on addition and subtraction at home. It goes from first counting all the way to adding and subtracting past 100 with regrouping, plus number bonds and place value.
  • Your child melts down over wrong answers. There's no buzzer and no shame. A wrong answer gets gentle encouragement, and a right one lights up a little planet.
  • You want a quick, low-prep session. There's a daily challenge, and the 46 question types level up on their own, so you don't have to pick the difficulty.
  • You don't want ads or sign-ups in front of your kid. No ads, no account, no login, and links out of the app sit behind a parent gate.
  • You like to know where they're stuck. The Parent Zone shows five skill areas, flags weak spots, and starts focused practice in one tap.

Probably not the right fit

  • You need a full curriculum or lesson plan. It's practice, not a full course with lessons.
  • You want timed fact drills. The listing describes it as building thinking rather than drilling speed. If beating the clock is the goal, look elsewhere.
  • Your child is working on fractions, decimals or pre-algebra. The listing doesn't mention those. It names addition and subtraction past 100, multiplication, division, number bonds, place value, patterns, time and money.
  • You want an online account for tracking progress. There's no account or login, and the listing says nothing leaves the device. What you do get is the Parent Zone and a learning report you can print or save as a PDF.
  • You want zero screen time for math. Totally fair. Counting at the grocery store and paper practice work great.

Common questions

Is Lumi Math Planet good for a child who can't read yet?

Yes, that's who it was built around. Every question is visual and read aloud, so a pre-reader can play without help sounding out words.

Will my kid see ads?

No. The App Store listing states there are no ads, no account or login, and no data collected. Links that lead out of the app sit behind a parent gate.

Is it a replacement for school math or a tutor?

No. Think of it as a short daily practice that fits around what your child is already learning. It isn't a teacher or a full course.

What can my child play without buying?

The first planet and the daily challenge are included without buying. The rest of the galaxy unlocks with a single one-time in-app purchase. There's no subscription.

Can I print practice pages from the app?

Yes. From the Parent Zone you can export a printable worksheet and a learning report, then print them or save a PDF. The listing says worksheets come with answer keys.
